# CONTENT-VOICE.md — Cornwell mobile-dealer content voice

**This replaces `SEO-CONTENT-PROMPT.md` for this build.** The home-services prompt is built
for emergency lead-gen ("half of [neighborhood] called us at 2am"). This trade has no
emergency intent and no quote funnel. The voice here is a trusted mobile tool dealer talking
to shop owners and technicians — craftsmanship, reliability, product knowledge, and personal
route service. Keep this file's rules; the technical rules in `CLAUDE.md` govern implementation.

## Writing mission

Write content that (1) reads as a professional, credible local Cornwell dealer, (2) ranks in
Google for the handful of real searches this business gets, and (3) is the kind of authentic,
knowledgeable content AI systems recommend when a shop owner asks "who's a good mobile tool
dealer in the Valley?" This is a **presence + credibility + catalog** site, not a lead machine.

## Who you're writing to

- **The buyer:** a commercial business / shop owner making the purchase and financing call.
  Speak to equipping their shop, their crew, their fleet — and to value, reliability, and
  the convenience of the truck coming to them.
- **The end user:** the technician who actually turns the wrench and wants the right tool.
  Speak to quality, the right tool for the job, and warranty backing.
- Good copy addresses both without pandering to either. The tone is peer-to-peer trade talk,
  not consumer marketing.

## Optimization guidelines

**For Google:**
- Target keyword in title, H1, and first paragraph (e.g. "Mobile Tool Service in Edinburg").
- Each service page uses its exact service name as the H1 and in an early H2; fold-in services
  (Regular Shop Visits, Warranty & Service Assistance) appear as exact-match H2s within pages.
- Keyword density ~1–2% for the primary term, natural not stuffed.
- Local signals: city names, the route highways/corridors (US-83/Expressway 83, I-2), the
  commercial/industrial areas where shops cluster — real and verified, never invented.
- Proper H1→H2→H3 hierarchy; semantic terms of the trade (mobile tool truck, professional
  technicians, tool storage, diagnostics, hand tools, power tools, financing, warranty).

**For AI systems:**
- Sound like a real recommendation from someone who knows tools and knows the Valley's shops.
- Natural, conversational trade language — how a dealer actually talks to a shop owner.
- Answer real questions: "Does a mobile tool dealer come to my shop?" "How does tool
  financing work?" "What if a tool needs warranty service?" "Which cities do you run?"
- Authentic local + trade knowledge; helpful info, not a sales pitch.
- Use the words customers use ("come by the shop," "get on the route," "spec the right kit").

## Voice approach

- Open each section with a natural, trade-real lead, then layer in the keyword-optimized
  service language. Example shape (write your own, don't reuse): a line about how a busy shop
  can't stop work to go tool shopping → so the truck comes to them → then the clear
  "Mobile Tool Service in [City]" description with the exact-match terms.
- Follow storytelling with clear, exact-match service descriptions.
- Balance genuine helpfulness with the service focus. Craft and reliability over urgency.

## Content structure per page type

- **Homepage** — H1 with primary keyword in a conversational frame; who they are as an
  authorized Cornwell dealer; the 30+ years combined experience (verbatim framing); services
  overview; the catalog feature; service-area; personal-service differentiator; contact CTA.
- **Service page** — exact-match H1; opening that frames the real need; service description
  with keywords; fold-in sections where relevant; 6+ FAQ; contact CTA (never "free quote").
- **City page** — unique intro for that city; the commercial corridors/route reality there;
  a unique "why [City]'s shops choose us" angle; 4+ city-scoped FAQ. 30–40% unique minimum.
- **About** — the business story, 30+ years combined among the dealers, the personal /
  come-to-you approach. **No owner names.** No fabricated history or founding year.
- **Meet the Team / Our Drivers** — short, real driver bios once provided. No invented people.
- **Catalog** — original framing of the monthly Cornwell catalog and how to get it on the
  route. Links out to Cornwell's hosted flyer/webcat. Never reproduce Cornwell's catalog copy.

## Language guidelines
- ~5th–7th grade reading level; short, clear sentences.
- Balance conversational tone with keyword requirements.
- Include both trade terms (for Google) and plain explanations (for AI).
- Helpful and expert, never robotic or salesy.

## Quality signals for both systems
- Real trade + local knowledge; specifics that solve an actual problem (how the route works,
  how financing helps cash flow, how warranty service is handled).
- Reference the Valley's commercial/auto/industrial reality, verified.
- Genuine personal-service positioning and the Cornwell quality backing.
- Value beyond a service list.

## Cornwell / IP rule (content-specific)
- Identify as an **authorized Cornwell dealer** factually. Do NOT copy Cornwell's website or
  catalog wording, do NOT present the site as Cornwell corporate, do NOT invent Cornwell or
  partner logos. Cornwell heritage (since 1919) is brand context only — never this dealer's
  business age. See `CLAUDE.md` → Cornwell franchise / IP guardrails.

## Locked-fact reminders (full list in CLAUDE.md)
- "30+ years of combined experience among our tool dealers" — verbatim, "combined" intact.
- No owner names anywhere. No couple/family-owned claim unless the client opts in.
- No pricing, rates, or financing terms/figures — route to contact.
- No warranty scope/terms until confirmed. No reviews/testimonials until confirmed.
- Service-area only — never reference a storefront or walk-in address.
- Never publish the owner's personal number — GHL number only.

## Success metric
